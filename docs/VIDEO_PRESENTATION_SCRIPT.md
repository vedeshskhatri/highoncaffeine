# THERMA — Video Presentation Script (7 to 8 Minutes)

*Read this script out loud naturally at a steady, relaxed pace. It takes approximately 7:30 to 7:45 minutes to read.*

---

Hello everyone, and thank you for your time.

Imagine standing at an Indian Army forward post in Ladakh or Siachen, 18,000 feet above sea level. Outside, the wind howls, and the temperature drops past minus thirty degrees Celsius.

Right now, most of the shelters our soldiers live in are basic corrugated tin sheds. Tin is a terrible insulator. It bleeds heat almost immediately into the freezing air. So to keep our soldiers alive through the bitter night, we burn kerosene in unvented metal stoves called bukharis.

That creates two massive problems.

First, it is dangerous. The smoke fills the room with black soot and carbon monoxide, which leads to chronic lung problems and severe hypothermia if a heater goes out in the middle of the night.

Second, it is an astronomical logistical burden. Getting just one single litre of kerosene up to Siachen by helicopter costs nearly two thousand four hundred rupees. Our armed forces spend thousands of crores every single year just flying fuel across mountain passes to keep soldiers from freezing.

So we asked ourselves a very simple question: What if our border outposts didn't need to burn a single drop of fuel to stay warm?

That is why we built THERMA.

Here is the paradox of the Himalayas: the ambient air is brutally cold, but because the atmosphere is so thin at high altitudes, the solar radiation is among the strongest on Earth. It is blindingly bright up there.

The problem has never been a lack of heat. The problem is that our shelters haven't been designed to capture that free energy and hold onto it.

THERMA is an area-specific architectural platform. It takes the exact elevation, local weather patterns, and sun angles of any Himalayan post, and it designs passive solar shelters that stay comfortably warm—at plus eighteen degrees Celsius—purely using sunlight, local materials, and smart insulation.

Let's take a look at the live platform.

The moment you open THERMA, you are brought directly into our 3D Open Shelter Studio.

Right in the center of the screen, you see a full three-dimensional interactive model of a defense outpost designed for Siachen Base Camp.

On the right side of the screen, our inspector panel gives engineers complete architectural control. We can adjust the dimensions, the roof pitch, the doors, and the building envelope.

If you look at the south-facing wall, you will see large double-pane glass windows. Directly behind that glass is a thick, three-hundred-millimeter dense stone wall—what architects call a Trombe wall.

During the day, the intense Himalayan sun passes straight through the glass and gets absorbed into that stone. Because stone has high thermal mass, it acts like a giant natural battery. It soaks up heat all afternoon, and then, when the sun sets and the outside air freezes, that stone slowly radiates gentle warmth into the living quarters for the next eight to twelve hours.

At night, automated insulation shutters close over the glass to prevent heat from leaking back out into the cold night sky.

And we didn’t design this with exotic, expensive imported parts. Every layer uses materials already available in Ladakh—local granite stone, compressed earth, and rockwool insulation—meaning border roads convoys can easily transport and build them locally.

Now, let’s test this in harsh reality.

Right from the studio, we can run a full twenty-four-hour thermal simulation for a freezing January night.

When you look at the simulation curve, the difference is night and day. In conventional tin barracks, once the kerosene stove dies down, the room temperature collapses. By four o'clock in the morning—the coldest hour of the diurnal cycle—the tin shed drops all the way down to minus eighteen degrees Celsius. That is deep hypothermia territory.

Now look at the THERMA curve. Even when the outside air plunges below minus twenty-eight degrees, our passive shelter holds steady at plus eighteen degrees Celsius.

It stays completely safe and comfortable through the coldest hours of the morning, without burning kerosene and without running a noisy diesel generator. That means our troops get a restful, warm night's sleep with clean air, waking up ready for duty.

Now, let’s zoom out from a single building to the entire defense sector.

In our side navigation, you can jump straight to the Site Registry. Our armed forces don't just protect one location; they manage a vast network of outposts across the Himalayas—from Daulat Beg Oldie in the far north, down to Galwan, Pangong Tso, Chushul, and Nyoma.

Each of these outposts has completely different elevations and terrain. A shelter designed for three thousand meters in Leh won't work at over five thousand meters on the Karakoram pass. THERMA automatically evaluates every registered site, pulling live terrain and solar data, and calculates the exact wall thickness, glass area, and thermal mass needed for that specific location.

Next, let's look at another critical operational feature: our Cold Snap Early Warning Console.

Even with great passive buildings, extreme Himalayan blizzards can strike with very little warning. When that happens, commanders need to make rapid logistical decisions.

Our console scans forward seven-day weather forecasts across every outpost in the sector. Right at the top, a command ribbon gives an immediate executive summary: how many outposts are in danger of dropping below safe temperatures, how many troops are affected, and how much emergency buffer fuel is required.

More importantly, it connects the weather forecast to mountain pass accessibility. If heavy snow is about to close critical passes like Zoji La or Khardung La, the system flags the corridor vulnerability days in advance.

Commanders don't have to sift through pages of confusing charts. With our triage board, they can see every post at a glance, click on any outpost to review the exact logistics mandate, and acknowledge the dispatch directive in a single click before the roads get snowed in.

To wrap up: THERMA is not just an architectural design tool. It is an end-to-end operational platform built specifically for high-altitude defense engineering.

By replacing dangerous kerosene heaters with free, abundant solar energy, we eliminate millions of litres of airlifted fuel, save hundreds of crores in hazardous logistics flights, and most importantly, we protect the lives, health, and operational readiness of the brave soldiers guarding our nation’s northern borders.

Thank you very much for your time, and I am happy to answer any questions.
