# THERMA — 7 to 8 Minute Video Presentation Script
**Project:** DRDO PS 26051 · Smart India Hackathon 2026  
**Topic:** Area-Specific High-Altitude Passive Solar Shelter Design  
**Target Duration:** 7:30 – 8:00 Minutes (~1,100 Spoken Words at 140 WPM)  
**Tone:** Conversational, confident, natural, and grounded in real-world military defense reality. Zero confusing academic jargon.

---

## Quick Recording Checklist Before You Start
1. Open browser in full screen at `http://localhost:5173/` (Landing Page).
2. Ensure your dev server is running smoothly (`npm run dev` and backend `uvicorn`).
3. Set your browser zoom to 100% and keep your mouse movements calm and deliberate.
4. Speak naturally, as if you're explaining a breakthrough solution to a colleague or senior officer.

---

## Complete Timed Script

### [0:00 – 1:15] SECTION 1: The Problem — Surviving the Himalayan Winter
**Target Time:** 1 Minute 15 Seconds (~175 Words)  
**On-Screen Action:**
- Start on the **Landing Page** (`http://localhost:5173/`).
- Slowly scroll down to highlight the statistic: *"It costs ₹2,400 to lift one litre of fuel to Siachen."*
- Pause on the headline and the hero pillars.

**Spoken Dialogue:**
> "Hi everyone. Imagine standing at an Indian Army outpost in Ladakh or Siachen, 18,000 feet above sea level. Outside, the wind howls at minus thirty degrees Celsius.
>
> Right now, the shelters our soldiers live in are mostly corrugated tin sheds. Tin is a terrible insulator—it bleeds heat almost instantly. So how do our troops stay alive through the night? They burn kerosene inside unvented bukharis and metal stoves.
>
> That creates two massive problems. First, it fills the air with black soot and dangerous carbon monoxide, which leads to chronic respiratory issues and severe hypothermia risk if a burner fails. Second, it creates an enormous logistical nightmare. Getting one single litre of kerosene up to Siachen by helicopter costs nearly two thousand four hundred rupees. Our armed forces spend thousands of crores every year just flying fuel across mountain passes.
>
> We asked a very simple question: what if our outposts didn't need fuel to stay warm at all?
>
> That's why we built **THERMA**."

---

### [1:15 – 2:00] SECTION 2: The Solution — The Himalayan Solar Paradox
**Target Time:** 45 Seconds (~110 Words)  
**On-Screen Action:**
- On the landing page, scroll to the **Diurnal Keyframe Section** showing the 24-hour thermal graph.
- Point cursor to the sunny daytime curve vs the freezing night.

**Spoken Dialogue:**
> "Here’s the fascinating thing about Ladakh. The air is freezing, but because the atmosphere is so thin at high altitude, the solar radiation is among the highest on the planet. It’s blindingly bright.
>
> The problem has never been a lack of heat—it's that we haven't designed shelters to capture and hold it.
>
> THERMA is an area-specific architectural platform. It takes the local geography, solar angles, and weather data of every frontier sector, and designs passive solar shelters that stay comfortably warm—at plus eighteen degrees Celsius—purely using sunlight, local thermal mass, and smart insulation.
>
> Let’s jump directly into the live platform and see how it works."

---

### [2:00 – 3:45] SECTION 3: Live Demo — The 3D Open Shelter Studio
**Target Time:** 1 Minute 45 Seconds (~240 Words)  
**On-Screen Action:**
- Click **"Explore 3D Shelter Studio"** or **"Enter Platform"**.
- The full 3D Shelter Studio loads (`/sites/site_siachen_base/design`).
- Orbit the 3D shelter with the mouse to show the walls, solar glazing, and roof.
- In the right-hand **Inspector Panel**, point out the location: **Siachen Base Camp (5,400m)**.
- Demonstrate changing a parameter: click the South Wall or adjust the window size or orientation slider from South to West, and show the model update.
- Mention the materials: Point to the Trombe stone wall and Rockwool insulation layer.

**Spoken Dialogue:**
> "The moment you enter the platform, you’re greeted by our flagship tool: the **3D Open Shelter Studio**.
>
> Right in front of us is a real-time three-dimensional model of an eight-man frontier shelter tailored specifically for Siachen Base Camp.
>
> On the right, our Inspector gives engineers full architectural control. We can adjust the shelter dimensions, roof slope, and most importantly, the envelope materials.
>
> Notice how the south-facing wall has large double-pane glass panels. Behind that glass is a three-hundred-millimeter dense stone wall—a Trombe wall. During the daylight hours, the sun passes through the glass and gets absorbed directly into the stone.
>
> Because stone has high thermal mass, it absorbs the heat slowly during the day. Then, when the sun goes down, it releases that stored warmth into the living quarters inside over eight to twelve hours, acting like a giant natural radiator.
>
> At night, automated insulation blankets slide over the glass to stop heat from escaping back into the cold sky. Every single building layer uses materials available in the region—local granite stone, compressed earth, and rockwool—so border roads convoys can actually transport and construct them."

---

### [3:45 – 5:00] SECTION 4: 24-Hour Simulation & The 04:00 AM Thermal Crash
**Target Time:** 1 Minute 15 Seconds (~180 Words)  
**On-Screen Action:**
- In the top navigation bar of the 3D Studio, click the **"Simulate"** button or step `02 Simulation`.
- Show the 24-hour diurnal temperature curves graph rendering on screen.
- Move the cursor along the timeline to **04:00 AM (Dawn Crash)**.
- Highlight the comparison: Outdoor ambient drops to -28°C; uninsulated tin crashes to -18°C; but the THERMA shelter holds steady at +18.0°C.

**Spoken Dialogue:**
> "Now, let’s test this in harsh reality. I'm going to run a twenty-four-hour thermal simulation for a freezing January day.
>
> Watch this curve. In conventional tin barracks, as soon as the stove dies out at midnight, the indoor temperature plummets. By four o'clock in the morning—the coldest hour of the day—the tin barracks crash to minus eighteen degrees Celsius. That’s hypothermia territory, where troops wake up shivering and disoriented.
>
> But look at the red line: our THERMA passive shelter. Even when the outside temperature drops below minus twenty-eight degrees, the inside room temperature never dips below plus eighteen degrees Celsius.
>
> It stays safe, comfortable, and warm throughout the entire night without burning a single litre of kerosene or running an electric generator. That means zero carbon monoxide, zero fire hazard, and completely uninterrupted rest for our troops."

**Target Time:** 1 Minute (~140 Words)  
**On-Screen Action:**

- Show the **Sidebar Navigation**: point out that **3D Shelter Studio** is right at the top under PLATFORM.

**Spoken Dialogue:**

>
> Notice in our side navigation, the **3D Shelter Studio** is always our primary station right at the top.
>
> If we click over to the **Site Registry**, commanders get an instant bird’s-eye view of every single outpost in the sector—from Daulat Beg Oldie in the north to Chushul and Hanle on the eastern high plateau.
>
> Every post has unique elevations and weather patterns. A shelter design that works at three thousand metres in Leh won't survive at five thousand metres on the Karakoram pass. THERMA automatically adapts each shelter’s wall thickness and solar glazing based on the precise latitude and solar irradiance of that specific outpost."

### [6:00 – 7:00] SECTION 6: Cold Snap Early Warning & Contingency Console
**Target Time:** 1 Minute (~150 Words)  
**On-Screen Action:**
- Click on **"Cold Snap Alerts"** (`/alerts`) in the sidebar.
- Show the newly built **Triage Cockpit**:
  - Point to the **Executive KPI Ribbon**: 11 Active Directives, 5,200 L Kerosene Buffer Needed, 132 Troops at Risk, 2 Passes Vulnerable.
  - Click on a row in the **Triage Board** (e.g. Tangtse or Siachen) to show the **Outpost Dossier Inspector** update on the right.
  - Point to the **Mandatory Action** and the live pass status (Zoji La & Khardung La).
  - Point to the **"Acknowledge All"** button.

**Spoken Dialogue:**
> "Finally, let’s look at our operational early-warning system: the **Cold Snap Contingency Console**.
>
> Even the best shelters need logistical support when an extreme arctic blizzard rolls in. This console connects directly to high-resolution seven-day weather forecasts.
>
> Right across the top, commanders see the big picture at a glance: eleven outposts are facing severe cold fronts, requiring fifty-two hundred litres of buffer fuel for one hundred thirty-two troops.
>
> But here’s the smart part: it correlates the weather with mountain road access. If a pass like Khardung La or Zoji La is about to close due to avalanche risk, the system alerts command two days in advance.
>
> By clicking any outpost in this triage board, an officer can see the exact dispatch mandate, verify the road corridor, and acknowledge the directive in one single click. It turns raw weather forecasts into life-saving military decisions before the roads get cut off."

## Helpful Tips for a Smooth Recording
- **Pacing**: Don't rush through the numbers. Speak at a relaxed, measured pace.
- **Cursor Discipline**: Only move the mouse when pointing to something specific on screen. Avoid circling randomly.
- **Transitions**: When switching between the 3D Studio and the Alerts Console, give the screen half a second to settle before speaking the next sentence.
